Keep for Meta: your glasses capture, your phone remembers

Keep becomes the memory layer for Meta AI. Meta glasses, or a small Keep pendant, capture a moment with one deliberate tap. The phone receives it, works out who was there and why it matters, and files it on Keep's calendar, where Meta AI can answer questions about it later.

Builds on: Keep prototypeAnswers: mentor critiqueReference: OpenAI × Hardware "Tomo" case study

The proposal in one minute

  • Capture lives on the face, not the phone. Meta glasses are the primary input. A tap on the temple saves a moment: a photo, a short clip or a voice note, plus time and place.
  • A pendant for people who don't wear glasses all day. The Keep Charm is a small clip-on with a camera, microphone and one button. It's a fashion accessory first, styled like a Ray-Ban charm.
  • The phone is the receiving point. Everything streams to the Keep app, which already has the calendar, the swipeable memories and "Ask your memories." Nothing is processed on the wearable.
  • Meta AI is the brain. Asking "What did Jake say about the boat?" goes to Meta AI with the relevant memories attached. The answer shows the original photo or clip.
  • It pays off on day one. Onboarding imports your people and your goals, so the very first capture already connects to something you care about.

Why now: Meta's Wearables Device Access Toolkit 1.0 began rolling out on September 30, 2026. It gives a phone app access to the glasses' camera, microphone and speakers over Bluetooth, with photo capture and video streaming up to 720p at 30fps. That's exactly the "phone as receiver" setup this concept needs, so the glasses half is buildable today.

How data moves

Capture

Meta glasses or Keep CharmTap to save a moment. Hold to talk. The capture light is on whenever it records.

Receive

Keep on the phonePulls photos, clips and audio over Bluetooth through Meta's toolkit, and stamps them with time, place and who's nearby.

Understand

Meta AI + your contextTranscribes, matches the people you've named, links to your goals, and files it on the day.

Everything lands on Keep's calendar → swipe through the day → ask your memories

Keep's existing screens map onto this directly. The camera landing page becomes a Devices view showing what the glasses or charm just sent. The day sheet and swipe view show the moments. "Ask your memories" becomes the Meta AI entry point.

Who it's for

The brief was broad, so we narrow it to people in their first year in a new city who already wear Meta glasses. They're defined by a situation and a habit, not an age:

  • Lots of new people, fast. Names, faces and "what did they do again?" are the main thing they forget, and the people graph solves exactly that.
  • Lots of firsts worth keeping. First apartment tour, first coffee spot, first friend dinner. Each one feels small at the time and meaningful a year later.
  • Early adopters. They already own the hardware, are comfortable with Meta AI, and are the people Meta wants to show new uses to.

Why not creators? Vloggers capture a lot, but much of it is performed for an audience. Their goal is finding footage to publish, not remembering their own lives, which makes it a different product. We'd keep them as a later segment for a "find that clip" feature.

Portfolio angle: designing for a problem Meta is actively exploring, with a working prototype on their real developer toolkit, is a strong signal to Meta and to wearable-AI startups.

The Keep Charm

A pebble about the size of a coin, worn on a chain, clipped to a collar or hung from a bag. It isn't an always-on recorder. It captures only when you press it, which keeps the battery small and the people around you comfortable.

Size
About 28 × 32 mm, 12 g
Capture
Press: photo. Press and hold: voice note. Double press: 10-second clip.
Signal
Light ring glows while capturing, visible from the front
Link
Bluetooth to the phone; larger clips transfer when the phone app is open
Wear
Magnetic back: necklace, collar clip or bag charm
Maker
Pitched as a Ray-Ban × Meta accessory, sold beside the glasses

Why Meta would want to make it

  • A cheaper door into the ecosystem. Someone who isn't ready for glasses can still start capturing with Meta AI, then upgrade later.
  • Fashion first. Ray-Ban's maker, EssilorLuxottica, already builds Meta's glasses. A charm fits their jewellery and accessories business.
  • Privacy by design. Button-only capture with a visible light avoids the "always recording" fear that has followed other AI pendants.
  • Same software path. The phone app receives charm captures the same way it receives glasses captures, so Keep doesn't need a second pipeline.

Privacy and social comfort

Our mentor flagged this as the core design challenge for glasses. These are our rules:

  • Deliberate capture only. No background recording. Every moment starts with a tap, press or voice command, and the capture light is always on while recording.
  • Only your people get names. Face matching runs on the phone and only against people you've named. Anyone else stays an unnamed face and is blurred if you share the moment.
  • Explain why it captured. When Keep saves something on its own suggestion, a notification says why ("Saved because you said 'remember this'").
  • Easy forgetting. One swipe deletes a moment everywhere. A "just us" switch keeps a day off the record entirely.

How we'd validate it

  1. Recruit 6 to 8 Meta glasses owners who moved cities in the last year. Hackathon networks and Phoenix's design community are a good start.
  2. Four weeks of normal life with Keep. Glasses capture through the toolkit; we hand-make a few charm prototypes from 3D-printed shells around an off-the-shelf camera module.
  3. Weekly recall quiz. Ask each person five questions about their own week, first from memory, then with Keep. Track how often Keep is right, and how often it adds something they'd forgotten.
  4. Watch the first week. Captures per day, day-7 return rate, and whether the first-capture insight felt helpful or creepy.

How it differs from Tomo

The OpenAI × Hardware case study made a strong case that memory is the moat, and explored a pendant feeding ChatGPT's "Moments." We take that idea further in three ways:

  • Hardware that already exists. Glasses-first means people can use it now, through Meta's real toolkit, not only in renders.
  • People and goals, not just moments. The people graph and imported context are what make day one useful.
  • One audience. New-city early adopters shape every decision, from onboarding to the recap.

What's real and what's assumed

  • AvailablePhone apps can use Meta glasses' camera, microphone and speakers through the Device Access Toolkit (1.0 rolling out from September 30, 2026; iOS and Android).
  • AvailableMeta AI on the glasses already remembers things you ask it to ("remember where I parked").
  • AssumedThat Keep can pass memories to Meta AI as context for answers. That needs a Meta AI integration we haven't confirmed; the fallback is Keep's own search and an LLM we choose.
  • AssumedThe Keep Charm is a concept. Meta has not announced a pendant, and the specs above are design targets, not engineering numbers.
  • CheckToolkit availability varies by country, and face recognition has legal limits in places such as Illinois and the EU. On-device matching against named people only is our starting position, not legal advice.
